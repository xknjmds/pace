Slide 2 · Problem
7:41 AM. Class starts at 8:00. Baby just woke up.

Meet Baby. 7:41 AM. Class starts at 8:00. She just woke up. Pause.

She skips breakfast, runs, and walks in at 8:15. The quiz has started. And the lab report due at 9? She only remembered it at 11 last night.

Baby is made up, but this morning isn't: it's ours. And about three in four college students call themselves procrastinators.

Signpost: “We've named the problem. Here's the workflow we propose.”

Slide 3 · Solution
PACE turns a guessed morning into a planned one.
0:35

Here's the change. Today, wake time is a guess, you leave when you notice the clock, deadlines show up the night before, and one bad day resets your streak.

With PACE, you enter your class time once, and it works out when to wake, eat and leave, with a reminder at each step. If you're late, it shows one thing: “Leave now.” Deadlines get four reminders that come closer. And on a bad day, low-energy mode shows only what's close. No streaks, no red.

That's our key difference: PACE does the planning, and it forgives.

Signpost: “And this isn't a mock-up. Here's the app.”

Slide 6 · Business model
Students pay ₱49 a month for Plus. The planner stays free.
0:25

Who pays? The student, or a parent. How often? ₱49 a month, or ₱399 a year. Why? So you never lose your plan: sync and backup across your phone and laptop.

The planner itself stays free forever, because the students who need it most shouldn't hit a paywall. Tiimo, a similar app, does the same; about one in ten of its users pay. Our price is an assumption, and we'll test it.

Signpost: “Before any forecast, here's how we'll prove it.”

Presents: Japheth Bayogos

quiz
Slide 7 · Validation
Evidence before forecasts: three tests, starting week 1.
0:35

We don't have customer data yet, so here's exactly how we'll get it. Three tests, each one could prove us wrong.

Problem proof, week 1: survey all 30 classmates and interview 8. Are they late to first class at least once a week? That number is our baseline.

Use proof, week 2: can 10 classmates set up their week alone in under five minutes?

Business proof, weeks 3 to 10: a pilot with all 30 classmates. Are they still using it in week 8? Are they late less often than at baseline? Will they pre-order Plus?

Pause after “each one could prove us wrong.”

Slide 8 · ROI
Plus pays for itself if PACE saves 1.4 minutes a school day.
0:25

Is ₱49 worth it? Here's an honest model, and every number on this slide is an assumption.

If PACE saves a student five minutes a school day of deciding and scrambling, that's 110 minutes a month. Valued at minimum wage, about ₱94 an hour, that's ₱172. Minus the ₱49 fee, that's ₱123 a month. Pause.

Even more simply: Plus pays for itself if PACE saves just 1.4 minutes a day. We'll measure the real number in the pilot. We left out grades and stress because we can't measure them fairly.
