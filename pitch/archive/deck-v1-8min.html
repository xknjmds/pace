This started with a real person: one of us. No daily system. Bedtime moving around, late to class, breakfast skipped, assignments remembered the night they were due, and on top of that, recovering from burnout.

Here's the thing: to-do apps store the due date, but they never tell you when to start. And habit apps reset your streak to zero the first day you slip, which feels terrible when you're already tired.

And it's not just us: about three in four college students call themselves procrastinators.

Why no streaks: in University of Delaware research, people whose broken streak was highlighted were less likely to keep going, especially when they blamed themselves (INSEAD Knowledge summary).

So here's our insight. People who are behind don't need more lists. They need one nudge at the right moment, and forgiveness when they miss it.

Most apps store tasks, reset streaks, and show you everything you missed. Research backs up what that feels like: when an app highlights a broken streak, people are less likely to keep going.

PACE flips each of those. It works out when to start, it counts what you did, and when you're late it gives you one step.

Keep going, 10 more Stop here

Deadlines get four reminders: three days before, the day before, the morning of, and two hours before. Each one is a little closer.

Two buttons make it kind. “Not today” quiets it for the day, and that isn't counted as a miss. “Submitted” cancels the rest.

And when it's time to work, there's Just start: a ten-minute timer. Starting is the hard part. After ten minutes you can keep going, or stop here, guilt-free.

Many mascots use guilt. Tuck only waves, rests, or quietly cheers.

This is what makes PACE different in how it feels.

Low-energy mode: on a bad day, every screen shrinks to just the next two days. No streaks: we count days done this week, and it never drops to zero. No red: being late is shown in a warm morning colour.

And Tuck, our turtle, has one rule: Tuck is never sad, never disappointed. That's the brand.

Good news, not a threat: Tiimo, a calm visual planner, has 500,000+ users and was Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025. People want kind planners. Comparison is our reading of public product pages, Nov 2026.

People always ask “isn't this just Todoist, or a calendar?”

Those apps store what and when it's due. Planners like Tiimo and Structured give you a nice timeline, but you still fill it in yourself. Habitica turns tasks into a game where you lose health when you miss.

PACE fills the morning in for you from one input, rebuilds it when you're late, and never punishes.

And Tiimo's success, half a million users and App of the Year, tells us people really want calm planners.
